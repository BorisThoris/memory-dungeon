# Original cards, late boards, and meteor pickups (rules 60)

The regular board, card artwork, matching rules, floor transitions, and camp remain the game at every depth. There is no separate constellation screen or focused-hand game.

Floors 1–60 keep their established sizes. After floor 60, the pair count doubles every 30 floors, up to 4,096 pairs / 8,192 actual cards. Larger decks reuse the original illustrations with a visible pair-number badge; both halves carry the same number. Historical rules retain the 24-pair cap and original pickup kind. This is a bounded real board, not an advertised million-card simulation.

The renderer visits a grid window with two cells of overscan. Boards of 48 or fewer cards retain their full renderer. On larger boards, up to 24 nearby cards use the full card materials, geometry and effects. Distant cards use the same artwork in instanced atlas pages of 64 cards, with 16, 32 or 64 pixel wide slots according to the visible population. Zooming materializes the original detailed cards in place. Hidden and removed cards retain their real game states, and raycasting resolves the actual card in each atlas instance. Card-specific high-resolution textures outside the committed window are disposed. Large boards skip the old whole-floor texture prewarm. Shared art, elemental backs, and materials remain cached. Atlas pages retain stable grid identities, so flipping one card only repaints the affected page. Large boards share tile lookup and hidden-pair indexes; automatic swap suggestions inspect a bounded set of candidates instead of every possible pair. Keyboard focus pans an offscreen card into view.

Meteor shards are 20% of pickup carrier pairs in new runs; score glints are the other 80%. Large decks retain at least one carrier pair per 24 pairs. Matching a shard adds one meteor to the run inventory. It never fires automatically. Use Meteor in the dock or Call meteor in Inventory, then select a card. Escape, clicking Meteor again, or selecting another tool cancels targeting without spending. A valid impact spends one charge and clears the area plus every affected card's partner. Destroyed shards do not grant new meteors, and the effect does not count as a manual match. Unused charges carry between floors; a new run starts with zero.

The impact uses one shader plane with a short trail, flash, ring and sparks. Reduced motion removes travel, expansion and sparks; the effect follows the board's pause-aware visual clock. Its rendering cost does not increase with the number of cards removed.

Validation includes a full 8,192-card generated board, pair-identity and survivor invariants, historical generation, inventory gain/spend, viewport working-set limits, instance picking, and isolated headless browser checks of the phone UI and large-board zoom.
