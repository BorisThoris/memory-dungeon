# The market, measured: how comparable products actually structure a run

Every number in this file was fetched directly from Steam's public global achievement statistics
and quoted from the page, product by product. Nothing here is an extracted claim awaiting
verification, and nothing is a developer's assertion about their own game. It is Valve's own
aggregate, across the whole owner base of each title.

Read the limits in §4 before using any of it. They are severe and they are the point.

## 1. The ladder-top convergence

Nine products, nine difficulty or completion ladders, and the top rung lands in the same narrow
band almost every time.

| Product | Top rung | Global % |
|---|---|---|
| Slay the Spire | Ascend 20 | **7.3%** |
| Dead Cells | Dracula (newest DLC boss) | **7.6%** |
| Monster Train | Win at Covenant 25 | **8.0%** |
| Hades | Reach the epilogue | **8.4%** |
| Binding of Isaac: Rebirth | Beat everything in Hard mode (Jacob and Esau) | **7.1%** |
| Balatro | Win on Gold Stake | **12.1%** |
| Peglin | Cruciball 20 | **1.9%** |
| Luck be a Landlord | Floor 20 | **12.4%** |

Five of the eight sit between 7.1% and 8.4%. That is a tight enough cluster, across five separate
studios and five unrelated ladder designs, to be worth treating as a planning figure: **roughly one
owner in twelve reaches the end of a roguelike's difficulty ladder.**

The two above the band (Balatro 12.1%, Luck be a Landlord 12.4%) are both short-session
score-chasers, which is this game's own shape. The one far below (Peglin 1.9%) has the longest
ladder of the set at 20 rungs with a boss fight at each.

Intermediate rungs, where published, decline smoothly rather than falling off a cliff:

- Slay the Spire: Ascend 0 **66.5%** → Ascend 10 **14.9%** → Ascend 20 **7.3%**
- Monster Train: Cov 5 **34.1%** → Cov 10 **20.6%** → Cov 15 **13.9%** → Cov 25 **8.0%**
- Peglin: Cruciball 5 **25.5%** → 10 **10.5%** → 15 **4.5%** → 20 **1.9%**
- Balatro: Red Stake **43.8%** → Black Stake **30.3%** → Gold Stake **12.1%**

One trap worth naming: Slay the Spire's "Ascend 0" at 66.5% is **not** evidence of elective
climbing. It is granted on a first win, so it measures "beat the game once". Only the 14.9% and
7.3% figures measure someone choosing to climb.

## 2. Dailies: who actually ships one, and who touches it

This is the question this project most needed, because it ships a daily, a streak and a shared-run
code on no evidence at all.

| Product | Daily challenge? | Reach |
|---|---|---|
| Slay the Spire | Yes | **24.1%** have won a Daily Climb |
| Dead Cells | Yes | **23.5%** have finished one daily |
| Monster Train | Yes | **23.4%** have *started* one |
| Balatro | **No daily achievement at all** | — |
| Vampire Survivors | **No daily achievement at all** | — |
| Binding of Isaac: Rebirth | **No daily achievement** (the mode exists) | — |
| Peglin | **No daily achievement** | — |
| Luck be a Landlord | **No daily achievement** | — |

Two things stand out.

**Where a daily is instrumented at all, its reach is remarkably consistent: 23.4%, 23.5%, 24.1%.**
Three different studios, three different genres, and about a quarter of owners touch the daily
once. That is a usable planning figure for reach.

**And the most commercially successful recent comparable — Balatro — ships no daily achievement,
nor an endless one.** Neither does Vampire Survivors. Their ladders are stakes and survival time
instead. This does not prove dailies are worthless; it does mean the two breakout short-session
score-chasers of recent years did not consider a daily worth an achievement slot, which is
evidence against treating one as obligatory.

Monster Train is the only product in the set that publishes daily *depth* rather than reach,
because its dailies are scored in tiers:

- Start a Daily Challenge: **23.4%**
- Score 30,000 in one: **11.7%**
- Score 40,000: **7.4%**
- Score 50,000: **3.6%**

So of the quarter who start a daily, roughly half get a scoring result and about one in six gets a
good one. The funnel inside the daily is as steep as the difficulty ladder outside it.

## 3. The one real retention curve in public data

Luck be a Landlord — a short-session score-chaser, the closest structural relative in this set —
instruments **repeat play** rather than one-time milestones. This is the only genuine engagement
curve available publicly anywhere in the survey:

| Wins | Global % |
|---|---|
| 5 | **41.1%** |
| 10 | **30.8%** |
| 25 | **8.1%** |
| 50 | **2.2%** |
| 100 | **0.9%** |
| 250 | **0.4%** |
| 500 | **0.3%** |
| 777 | **0.2%** |

The shape is the finding. It is not a smooth decay: between 10 wins and 25 wins the population
falls by nearly four times (30.8% → 8.1%), and after 50 it flattens into a long thin tail that
barely moves from 0.9% to 0.2% across a further 677 wins.

**Roughly 8% of owners play a short-session roguelike 25 times. About 2% play it 50 times.** The
"hundreds of hours" player is real but is a fraction of a percent, and everything built solely for
them is built for 0.2% of buyers.

Its floor ladder decays far more gently — Floor 1 **59.1%** to Floor 20 **12.4%**, losing only a
few points per rung — which suggests a ladder of many small steps holds people better than a
ladder of few large ones. That is a hypothesis this data supports, not something it proves.

## 4. What this data cannot do — read before citing any of it

**These are one-time lifetime unlocks.** Except for Luck be a Landlord's win counts and Monster
Train's score tiers, nothing here distinguishes a player who did something once from one who did
it five hundred times. They measure **reach**, not retention, not return, not session count.

So the honest answer to "do daily challenges retain players" is still: **this data cannot tell
you, and no source found in either research pass can.** What it can tell you is how many people
ever touch one, which is a different and smaller question.

**Absence of an achievement is not absence of a feature.** Binding of Isaac has a daily run mode
and no achievement for it. Hades has the Heat system and no achievement for it. Dead Cells' Boss
Stem Cell ladder — its flagship difficulty escalation — has **no achievement at all**, so it is
publicly unmeasurable. Where this file says "no daily achievement", it means exactly that and
nothing more.

**Owner base is not player base.** Bundles, sales and gifts put a game in libraries it is never
launched from, which drags every percentage down by an unknown amount that differs per title. The
cross-product comparisons are more trustworthy than the absolute values.

**Achievement design differs per game**, so "top rung" is not the same accomplishment across
products. Ascension 20 and Cruciball 20 and Gold Stake are different mountains.

## 5. What follows for this game

Stated as implications, not instructions, and each traceable to a number above.

- **A daily is worth about a quarter of owners touching it once.** If this game's daily costs more
  than that is worth, the two most successful comparables did without one.
- **Design the ladder for the 8%, but do not design the game for them.** If the top of this game's
  difficulty ladder is reached by roughly one owner in twelve, content gated behind it is content
  most buyers never see.
- **Many small rungs appear to hold better than few large ones** (Luck be a Landlord's twenty
  floors lose ~2.5 points a rung; Peglin's twenty Cruciballs lose far more).
- **The 25-run cliff is where a short-session roguelike loses people** — a four-fold drop between
  10 and 25 wins. That is the interval where content variety has to still be arriving.
- **This game currently ships a daily, a streak, a shared-run code, endless mode and a difficulty
  ladder.** Nothing here says any is wrong. It does say that only the daily has a measured reach
  figure attached, and that the burden of evidence for the rest has not been met by anything found
  in two research passes.
